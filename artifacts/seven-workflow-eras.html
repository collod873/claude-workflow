02

Era by era

What each system was genuinely good at, what it cost, and the specific thing that ended it — cited to the artifact that says so, not reconstructed.

ERA 1

Plain plan mode → implement

Mar 2026 →

Enter plan mode, agree a plan, exit, implement step by step. No enforcement, no artifact beyond the plan file.

What worked

  • Zero setup cost. 86 crewops commits in March alone, tagged plan(phase-1): step N — it shipped a multi-tenant CRM from an empty repo.
  • The plan file as the unit of work survived every later era. Step-numbered commits are still legible five months on.

What didn't

  • Nothing enforced anything. The lesson recorded in era 2's wiki is entirely about this: docs require a reader.
  • Plan-mode auto-names leaked the friction. Cockpit's .claude/plans/ holds 44 files including dont-use-spec-first-sequential-galaxy.md and i-dont-think-contradictions-cozy-moth.md — the objections are literally in the filenames.
  • Never retrospected. The only record is git log.
How it endedIt didn't. It's still the default for anything below ticket size — which is the honest reason era 4 and era 5 both lost ground.
ERA 2

Homegrown enforcement — checkbox hooks

Mar – Apr 2026

Plans had to carry - [ ] acceptance items; hooks enforced that they were authored correctly and ticked off. validate-plan.py, checklist-reminder.py, post-edit-validate.py, five always-on rules.

What worked

  • The single most durable idea in the whole record. "Enforcement at execution time is the only mechanism that survives brain off + memory wiped."
  • It is still running. checklist-reminder.py and post-edit-validate.py are live in settings.json today — five months and four systems later.
  • Fail-closed beat documentation every time it was tested.

What didn't

  • A known architectural hole, parked and never fixed. validate-plan.py fired only on ExitPlanMode, so /slice-authored files bypassed the gate entirely. The PostToolUse fix was specified and never shipped.
  • The wiki compile pipeline duplicated itself. verify-ship-workflow-discipline.md restates the same five facts roughly six times each — 98 lines that carry about 16 lines of content.
  • Documentation scattered across two wiki topics and a skills tree.
How it endedAbsorbed, not killed. Skills archived into era 4's build/skills/; hooks consolidated into ~/.agents/skills/hooks/. validate-plan.py itself was finally deleted in August 2026 — era 6 has tickets, not plan files, so its gate had nothing left to guard.
ERA 3

obra/superpowers — evaluated, not adopted

Apr 13 – 14 2026

A 15-agent research sweep feeding a three-way audit: Superpowers × Matt Pocock × Collin's own stack. 77-row file-by-file matrix with a citation and a verdict per row.

What worked

  • The best evaluation on record, by a distance. matrix.md cites file and line range for every row, and every NEW verdict is preceded by an explicit grep null-result.
  • It correctly refused to pick a winner. Superpowers had the prompt artifacts, Pocock the planning primitives, Collin the only enforcement layer. The output was a merge plan, not an adoption.
  • Cheap. Two days, and it set the agenda for the next five weeks.

What didn't

  • It bought 19 skills at once. Era 4's autopsy later blamed exactly this: 19 skills, an estimated 19–57 latent self-contradictions, no audit loop.
  • The reference clones were gitignored. Every parity claim in the matrix depends on external/, an uncommitted directory. OPEN-PROBLEMS #5.
How it endedBy design — it was never a running system. It became input #1 to era 4.
ERA 4

Planning-System — the spine

Apr 14 – May 20 2026

/spec-first → /plan → /slice → /build → /verify → /ship. Domain adapters, a complexity score that forces slicing, a declarative Definition-of-Done gate, a two-stage verifier, a bounded self-heal loop. 19 skills, 5 reviewer agents.

What worked

  • The DoD gate genuinely worked. G7 drove all four criteria to pass on real artifacts, then broke each one in turn: 4/4 refused correctly, no false negatives, no criterion firing for the wrong reason.
  • Verifier ≠ implementer. Deliberately separated to dodge confirmation bias. Ported forward into every era since.
  • Files: + Scope: lines on every step. The postmortem is explicit: knowing exactly which three files were in bounds "led to a smaller, more cohesive diff than a free-form implementation would have."
  • The most honest failure autopsy in the estate. Six numbered open problems and the line "Shipped, not settled."

What didn't

  • Ceremony exceeded the work. OPEN-PROBLEMS #4: seven plan steps for ~3 edits in one file. A ~7:3 ceremony-to-substance ratio, with no documented under-threshold bypass.
  • It was flagged a month before it died. Cockpit's 04-19 interview lists friction #4 as "spine ceremony vs plan-mode ambiguity — overkill for small tasks and unclear for medium ones."
  • Deferred decisions left live. Five demoted rules with no disposition; three stub adapters that look implemented.
  • The system had no maintenance mode. "Auditing costs as much as fixing. Every audit reveals more unconcluded experiments, not just bugs."
  • Two audiences, two doc shapes — Collin needs pending/risky; a fresh session needs live/don't-touch.
How it endedIt stopped being used. 128 of its 131 commits are April; after 04-28 it went quiet, and on 05-20 dfce23d archived the four homegrown standalones "alongside the retired spine." No dramatic failure — the gate worked and the ceremony around it didn't.
ERA 5

Sandcastle — the AFK pipeline

Jun 8 – Jul 2 2026

Grill → PRD → issues → label agent:implement → walk away. A 13-label state machine across 9 GitHub Actions workflows and 27 TypeScript ops, running on a 10-runner self-hosted pool. The most ambitious era by a wide margin.

What worked

  • It ran. Three repos on it; all 58 template PRs merged; CI green at the end. It did not fall over — it was switched off.
  • Best-instrumented era ever. Every op was a timed workflow run. Implement p50 13.6 min, review p50 7.6, PR open→merge p50 8.2 min — all still recoverable from the API two months later.
  • A real pre-merge gate. CI ran on the PR and blocked the merge (ADR-0006/0011).
  • Legible from GitHub alone. The issue list was the state.
  • The strongest decision discipline anywhere — 27 ADRs, each naming the incident and issue number that forced it.
  • Two ideas that outlived it: authoring-time physical disjointness (ADR-0017), and holding auto-merge on the human-owned doc surface (ADR-0026) — code lands itself, vocabulary and standards stay Collin's.

What didn't

  • Two thirds of the machine did nothing. 2,120 orchestration runs, 1,365 skipped. To-issues fired 299 times and skipped 299 times. Every label event woke every workflow.
  • Race whack-a-mole. Six ADRs to make one lockless surface safe. ADR-0014 quotes the complaint verbatim: the churn "reads like patching a model that keeps producing fresh holes" — then keeps the model.
  • Fixes relocated the problem. ADR-0017 killed the registry rebase storm; ADR-0020 opens with "the storm didn't die; it moved to the next shared registry of a different shape."
  • It could never see its own cost. Issue #39's verification gate failed — @ai-hero/sandcastle parses Claude Code's result line and discards total_cost_usd, duration_ms and num_turns. It ran to retirement blind to its spend.
  • Ceremony per unit of work. ADR-0023 measured one 8-slice PRD at 2h28m wall, ~650 compute-minutes, 79 workflow runs, waves of 2 on a 10-runner pool. p50 8 agent runs per PR; p90 16; max 32.
  • Security debt arrived in one batch. 26 issues on 06-22 including a CRITICAL pull_request_target running untrusted PR code with secrets on the runner, plus #111: "the 478-test suite doesn't cover the cross-workflow races that actually break."
How it ended — the reason nothing wrote downThe last day's work is the tell. On 07-02 the entire PRD (#146) was titled "Orchestration efficiency overhaul" — fat slices, review self-fix, doc budgets, auto-kick. The system's final act was undoing its own ceremony. It landed, and 2h17m later Lumaria deleted the pipeline. Only 2 issues were left open, so this was not collapse under a backlog: it was a judgement that the coordination cost was structural, not fixable. Upstream agrees — mattpocock/sandcastle itself has been dormant since 2026-06-29, three days earlier.
ERA 6

agent-skills — current

Jul 6 2026 → now

/grilling → /to-spec → /to-tickets → /drain → /implement, closed by a checker. Vendored mattpocock/skills with local deltas on top. 30 ADRs, a sync contract with executable markers, hooks symlinked from one home.

What works

  • 21× less machine time. 0.44 CI-hours/day vs Sandcastle's 9.5. 4.2 min per issue closed vs 34.
  • Cost is knowable for the first time. ~$1,661 API-equivalent over 28 days, itemised per model, session and day.
  • No coordination failure surface — because there's no coordination layer. Six race ADRs made moot.
  • UPSTREAM.md is the endpoint of the whole documentation trend — ~60 rows, each with a grep-able marker a test suite fails on. Deletions carry a "compensating addition," because a positive marker can't prove a clause stayed deleted.
  • Work the old model had no shape for. wayfinder alone is 3,709 attributed turns — open-ended exploration with the human present.
  • It learns from the record. ADR-0029 cites its own measured machinery ratio to shut off a chain it had just built.

What doesn't

  • The pre-merge gate is gone. All 12 CI failures since 08-16 are genuine unit+build breakage with zero flake — twelve broken commits reached main in five days. This is the one clean regression from era 5.
  • No per-ticket clock. Sandcastle got one free from workflow records; there is nothing comparable now.
  • The machinery is eating the capability. ADR-0029's own table: pipeline machinery was 63% of file touches in July and 82% in August. On 08-20, 20 of 92 commits were ratification verdicts against the hook harness that exists to enforce the standards.
  • Five of eleven open issues are /drain defects — worktrees exhausting tmpfs inodes, the checker moving HEAD in the foreman's shared checkout, checker evidence drifting from its own parser. Including #134: "a drain has nowhere to file defects it finds in the drain machinery itself."
  • Prompt overhead is real. A foreman hit 123k tokens having read zero implementation files — ~16k of it re-pasting the same two dispatch templates.
StatusLive, and by the numbers the best system yet on every axis except the merge gate. Note the shape it landed on is the same one Matt Pocock uses for his tooling repo: local Claude sessions, zero agent workflows.
ERA 7

Side branches

Apr – Jun 2026

Two experiments that ran alongside the primary system rather than replacing it.

What worked

  • Agent Teams shipped real work — 15 PRs landed overnight on Cockpit before it was abandoned.
  • The abandonment was empirical, not vibes. A uniq-marker probe proved Task-subagents do route through the parent's PermissionRequest hook and teammates don't. That one test picked the pattern every later era uses.
  • Crewops' /build-component proved project-scoped workflows can coexist with a global one.

What didn't

  • Agent Teams was structurally disqualified. Teammate permission requests bypass the hook layer entirely (anthropics/claude-code#23983), so auto-approve-permissions.py never fires — the exact opposite of era 2's durable lesson.
  • Mid-run correction doesn't stick. Hard-rule corrections sent to active builders "empirically do not stick — they forget by the next Bash call."
How it ended/agent-team deleted 2026-04-14 (Planning-System D9), replaced by Task subagents. Still the right call: era 6's /drain workers are that same pattern.

04

The seven recurring failures

Each of these shows up in three or more eras, independently, with different technology each time. That's what makes them properties of the operator and the work, not of any one system.

F1

Ceremony outgrows the work it wraps

The single most reliable failure. Every system that was replaced was replaced because its per-unit overhead stopped being worth it — never because it produced bad output.

  • Era 4Seven plan steps for ~3 edits in one file. ~7:3 ceremony-to-substance, no under-threshold bypass documented.
  • Era 1/4Cockpit's 04-19 interview names it a month before the spine dies: "overkill for small tasks and unclear for medium ones."
  • Era 5p50 8 workflow runs per PR, max 32. The final PRD is literally titled "Orchestration efficiency overhaul."
  • Era 6ADR-0029 deletes /drain's standards sweep because every landing paid for three fresh-context subagents even when there was nothing to say.
F2

The system becomes its own biggest customer

Past a certain size, the pipeline generates enough maintenance to consume the capacity it was built to create. This is measured, not felt.

  • Era 4"Auditing costs as much as fixing. Every audit reveals more unconcluded experiments, not just bugs." An estimated 19–57 latent contradictions with no detection mechanism.
  • Era 51,365 of 2,120 orchestration runs were no-ops. 26 issues filed in a single day auditing the pipeline against itself.
  • Era 6Machinery share of file touches: 63% in July, 82% in August. Five of eleven open issues are defects in /drain itself — and #134 exists because a drain has nowhere to file defects it finds in the drain.
F3

Fixes relocate the problem instead of removing it

The distinctive era-5 pathology, but not unique to it. A fix lands, the failure reappears one layer over, and the next ADR opens by conceding it.

  • Era 5ADR-0017 kills the aggregate-registry rebase storm. ADR-0020: "the storm didn't die; it moved to the next shared registry of a different shape" — the Drizzle migration chain.
  • Era 5ADR-0014 acknowledges the churn "reads like patching a model that keeps producing fresh holes," then deliberately keeps the model and documents its cost instead. That was the right call and it still didn't stop the churn.
  • Era 2The ExitPlanMode-only gate: the hole was known, the PostToolUse fix was specified, and it was never shipped in the era's lifetime.
F4

Deferred decisions are left live and look implemented

A parked call doesn't sit inert — it sits in the tree looking finished, and something downstream trusts it.

  • Era 4OPEN-PROBLEMS #1: five demoted rules with no disposition after seven days, three stub adapters that "look implemented but are placeholder folders."
  • Era 5#132 — the dispatcher committed runner slots with zero human in the loop, so every "decide during slicing" got silently improvised and surfaced only at review. Fixed by ADR-0019's closed-spec gate.
  • Era 6ADR-0027 generalises the answer: sensitive verdicts are flagged, not deferred. The call gets made and marked, never parked.
F5

Two sources of truth, and they drift

Every era grew a second description of one process, and every time the drift was discovered by someone following the wrong one.

  • Era 5ADR-0015: the README runbook and the /graft skill described one procedure and diverged. "Following the README literally on a real target does the exact damage the skill exists to prevent."
  • Era 2The wiki compile pipeline re-promoted the same facts 4–6× each, so the canonical topic reads as its own echo.
  • Era 6The direct answer: UPSTREAM.md markers asserted by test_upstream_markers.py, and ADR-0018's rule that seeded repo docs are the home and CLAUDE.md carries only a pointer. Documentation a test suite can fail on.
F6

Instrumentation is an afterthought, then it's gone

No era started with measurement. Two eras ended without ever being able to answer what they cost.

  • Era 5Issue #39's verification gate failed and the pipeline ran to retirement blind to its own spend — the SDK parses total_cost_usd and throws it away.
  • Era 6Has token cost but no per-ticket clock. Sandcastle got that free from workflow records; it was lost with the pipeline and nobody noticed for seven weeks.
  • AllSession capture stopped 2026-05-21 (SessionEnd: [] — deliberately emptied), and transcripts on this machine begin 2026-07-22. The record of five eras exists only because git and GitHub happened to keep it.
F7

Asking the human to size the work doesn't work

Four months to prove it, twice, in both directions.

  • Era 1/4Cockpit 04-19 designs a three-question pre-flight sizing helper precisely because "he can't pre-tell task size, he's not a coder."
  • Era 668b071f deletes it: the sizing questions "were senior-dev calls a human maintainer can't actually answer better than the breakdown's own author, and a headless run had to fake an answer to unblock itself anyway." Replaced by an audit agent.
  • Era 5Same shape from the other side — ADR-0019 exists because the pipeline let the agent improvise calls the operator owned. The fix in both cases is a machine judgement with a human-reviewable checkpoint, not a human quiz.
05

The six things that always worked

Ideas that were kept, ported, or independently re-derived across era boundaries. These are the assets — anything built next should start from them rather than rediscovering them.

W1

A gate that errors at the moment of the action

Stated in era 2 and never contradicted since: docs require a reader; a gate requires nothing but a trigger. It's the only claim in six months that every subsequent era validated rather than revised.

  • Proofchecklist-reminder.py and post-edit-validate.py, written March–April, are live in settings.json today — outlasting four systems that were supposed to supersede them.
  • Lineageclose-gate.py is era 2's insight applied to tickets: refuse to close without a posted Closing record with every acceptance criterion marked MET.
W2

The thing that checks is never the thing that built

Introduced in era 3, adopted in era 4 explicitly to avoid confirmation bias, and re-derived in every era since without anyone arguing for it.

  • Chainspec-reviewer → quality-reviewer (era 4) · review op + acceptance audit, ADR-0025 (era 5) · checker dispatched by stub, ADR-0011/0026 (era 6).
  • NoteEra 5's ADR-0024 refined it further: trivial findings self-fix, behavioural findings keep the loop. Worth porting into era 6's checker.
W3

Prevent conflicts at authoring time, not at merge time

The most valuable single invention of era 5, and it crossed the era boundary intact.

  • Era 5ADR-0017/0018: redraw slice boundaries so each owns its files; generate shared registries from per-slice declarations so no PR ever touches them.
  • Era 6/to-tickets runs a seam sweep first — one subagent per slice hunting an existing seam before proposing one — and enforces physical disjointness between parallel slices.
W4

Decisions live next to the code they govern

The clearest directional trend in the whole record: loose plan files → a separately-compiled wiki → in-repo ADRs → documentation a test suite fails on.

  • Counts27 ADRs (era 5), 30 ADRs (era 6) — each era's single best artifact, and each written incident-first with the issue number that forced it.
  • EndpointUPSTREAM.md: every divergence from upstream carries a grep-able marker, and deletions carry a compensating sentence that is itself the marker.
W5

Agents own code; the human owns vocabulary

Discovered in era 5 and kept, because it's the split that makes autonomy survivable.

  • Era 5ADR-0026: auto-merge holds any diff touching CONTEXT.md, CODING_STANDARDS, .claude/skills/ or CLAUDE.md. README and ADRs are agent-editable and don't trip the hold.
  • Era 6seeded-doc-router.py plus the CONTEXT.md glossary with _Avoid_ lists per term. Same boundary, no pipeline needed to hold it.
W6

Write the autopsy while it still stings

This analysis is only possible because two eras did this properly. It is also the habit that broke down exactly once — at era 5 — which is why #131 exists.

  • Era 4OPEN-PROBLEMS.md, four numbered friction postmortems (F/E/G/H series), and the line "Shipped, not settled."
  • Era 5ADRs that name their own churn in the opening paragraph — the reason its exit is reconstructable at all despite no postmortem.

The AFK premise was the part that didn't survive contact

Sandcastle existed to let Collin label something and walk away. Both eras land ~85% of commits inside waking hours, and the current model still commits at 02:37 via background drain workers. What actually changed is where the human sits: outside the loop applying labels, versus inside the session at 6.4 prompts per session. That's a preference, not a measurement — but it means the pipeline's founding justification was never really tested by the pipeline.

07

What the postmortem should say

Sandcastle didn't fail. It was correctly priced and declined.

It ran three repos, merged every PR, ended green, and had 2 issues open. What ended it was a judgement that the coordination cost was structural rather than fixable — and the evidence for that judgement is spread across ADR-0014 (the model produces fresh holes and we're keeping it anyway), ADR-0020 (the fix moved the problem), ADR-0023 (650 compute-minutes for 8 slices), and issue #39 (we cannot see what this costs). The final PRD being an efficiency overhaul is the system's own admission.

The one-line version: distributed orchestration was bought for a workload that was never distributed — one operator, one machine, one repo — and the replacement gets comparable throughput from local sessions in worktrees, where the coordination problem doesn't exist.

  1. Close #131 against the work already done. The autopsy, the A/B, and the CI performance doc were all written on 08-21 and are already committed. #131 should point at them and add the decision narrative above, not restart the investigation.
  2. Restore the pre-merge gate. Run pnpm check as a hard foreman step before a drain batch lands, rather than trusting each worker session to have run it. Twelve red commits in five days is the only unambiguous regression in the whole record, and this fixes it without re-introducing a pipeline.
  3. Add a per-ticket clock to /drain. Two timestamps — first agent action, merged into batch. It's a few lines and it restores the one genuinely useful thing Sandcastle gave for free.
  4. Decide on capture, formally. Right now the wiki is a detailed archive that silently stops in May and reads as current. Either re-wire SessionEnd on WSL or retire the wiki explicitly and commit to in-repo ADRs. The in-between state is what made this ticket hard.
  5. Watch F2 in the current system. 82% machinery share in August is the same curve era 4 and era 5 died on. ADR-0029 was the right instinct; it should not be the last cut.
  6. Archive the Sandcastle template or deprecate its README. It still reads as live — green CI, aspirational north star, no deprecation note, ten offline macOS runners still registered, and a README pointing at /Users/collinlodato/. The retirement commit's own logic applies to the template as much as it did to Lumaria's copy.
